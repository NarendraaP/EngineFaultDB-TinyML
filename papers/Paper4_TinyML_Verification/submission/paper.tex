\begin{abstract}
The deployment of deep learning models on resource-constrained microcontrollers (TinyML) is expanding rapidly, yet empirical evaluation in the field suffers from critical methodological vulnerabilities. Training-time in-memory metrics frequently diverge from disk-serialized binary behavior; theoretical weight sparsity is routinely conflated with physical storage compression; and host simulation timings are improperly extrapolated to microcontroller real-time guarantees. In this paper, we formulate an artifact-driven empirical verification protocol and defect taxonomy for compiled TinyML deployment artifacts. The protocol formalizes executable verification procedures across seven core dimensions: Data Isolation, Serialized Binary Integrity, Quantization Graph Inspection, Sparsity and Storage Decoupling, Computation Accounting, Timing Protocol Auditing, and Runtime Non-Leakage. We define formal programmatic predicates ($\mathcal{P}_1 \dots \mathcal{P}_7$) for each dimension and evaluate them on an end-to-end case study auditing 12 candidate neural network models trained on the 55,998-record EngineFaultDB physical benchmark. Our independent audit uncovers and resolves 20 distinct numerical discrepancies between training-time reports and verified disk binaries across four defect modes (with metric variances up to $7.82\%$). Furthermore, we empirically demonstrate that evaluating gating thresholds directly on test data introduces an artificial $+1.80\%$ optimistic accuracy bias in the evaluated pipeline, demonstrating the necessity of split-isolated calibration. By establishing formal verification predicates and classifying evidence into a 5-tier empirical framework, our protocol provides actionable software engineering procedures to support reproducible benchmarking and defensible deployment claims in edge AI research.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{E}{mbedded} Machine Learning and Tiny Machine Learning (TinyML) have experienced exponential growth, enabling intelligent inference on resource-constrained microcontrollers with sub-64\,KB SRAM and sub-256\,KB Flash budgets~\cite{warden2019tinyml,ray2022review,sanchez2020tinyml}. In applications ranging from automotive diagnostics to edge health monitoring, TinyML models must operate under strict physical memory, energy, and execution constraints~\cite{banbury2021benchmarking,dutta2021tinyml,sze2017efficient,liberis2021munas}.

However, the empirical literature in edge AI is increasingly vulnerable to reproducibility challenges and measurement discrepancies~\cite{pineau2021improving,kapoor2023leakage,henderson2018deep}. Unlike cloud-scale machine learning where training and deployment share homogeneous 32-bit floating-point execution environments, TinyML involves a complex, multi-stage software translation pipeline:
\begin{align*}
    \text{Keras/PyTorch} &\xrightarrow{\text{PTQ / Pruning}} \text{TFLite FlatBuffer} \\
    &\xrightarrow{\text{C-Array Export}} \text{TFLite Micro Runtime}
\end{align*}
At each transformation boundary, subtle discrepancies emerge between training-time abstractions and actual serialized binary behavior~\cite{david2021tensorflow,blalock2020state,lin2020mcunet}.

Recent surveys in machine learning software engineering have exposed systemic methodological traps~\cite{sculley2015hidden,amershi2019software}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{In-Memory vs. Binary Discrepancies:} Evaluating models in-memory during training loops rather than parsing the final serialized disk binary artifact.
    \item \textbf{Sparsity Conflation:} Assuming that zeroing $75\%$ of weights in a dense matrix automatically yields a $75\%$ reduction in serialized model file size or execution speedup.
    \item \textbf{Incomplete Quantization Graphs:} Claiming 8-bit integer inference while silent floating-point fallback operators remain in the runtime execution graph.
    \item \textbf{Timing Extrapolations:} Presenting single-sample host PC execution times as embedded Worst-Case Execution Time (WCET) or microcontroller hardware latency.
    \item \textbf{Threshold and Data Leakage:} Optimizing runtime routing thresholds on the test partition, producing optimistic accuracy estimates.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, this paper presents an \textbf{Artifact-Driven Empirical Verification Protocol and Defect Taxonomy for Compiled TinyML Deployment Artifacts}. We formalize verification criteria across seven dimensions of edge AI pipelines, establish executable verification predicates ($\mathcal{P}_1 \dots \mathcal{P}_7$), and demonstrate their efficacy through an exhaustive case study auditing 12 candidate models on the EngineFaultDB physical benchmark.

\textbf{Explicit Contributions:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A 7-dimensional verification taxonomy formalizing empirical quality gates across the TinyML deployment lifecycle.
    \item Seven formal, programmatic verification predicates ($\mathcal{P}_1 \dots \mathcal{P}_7$) with deterministic pass/fail execution procedures.
    \item An empirical case study auditing 12 candidate TinyML models across dense, quantized, pruned, and distilled paradigms.
    \item Identification, root-cause categorization, and resolution of 20 numerical discrepancies across four defect classes.
    \item An empirical demonstration of test-set threshold contamination producing a $+1.80\%$ optimistic accuracy bias.
    \item An explicit 5-tier evidence classification framework separating direct measurements, reproducible simulations, and derived operational estimates.
\end{enumerate}

We formulate four specific research questions (RQs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Discrepancy Prevalence \& Defect Modes):} How often and to what degree do independently recomputed TinyML metrics differ from reported values at training time, and what defect modes govern them?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Quantization Verification):} What structural properties in serialized FlatBuffers must be inspected to verify pure integer execution graphs without float fallback?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Sparsity vs. Storage Decoupling):} How does magnitude pruning affect theoretical active MACs versus physical on-disk FlatBuffer byte footprint in standard runtimes?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Leakage Prevention):} What quantitative bias is introduced by evaluating routing thresholds on test data, and how does split isolation eliminate it?
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Reproducibility in Machine Learning}
Pineau et al.~\cite{pineau2021improving} proposed the Machine Learning Reproducibility Checklist for NeurIPS, which focuses on code release, hyperparameter reporting, and statistical analysis. Kapoor and Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023leakage} performed a data leakage audit across 17 fields, finding extensive test set contamination. In the software engineering domain, Arcuri and Briand~\cite{arcuri2014hitchhiker} developed guidelines to evaluate randomized algorithms. However, these works focus on conventional ML and do not consider the unique embedded TinyML domain of hardware-software translation.

\subsection{TinyML Benchmarking and Systems Auditing}
Banbury et al.~\cite{banbury2021benchmarking} introduced MLPerf Tiny and defined benchmarking rules for latency, energy, and accuracy on microcontrollers. David et al.~\cite{david2021tensorflow} presented TensorFlow Lite Micro and described the interpreter internals. Blalock et al.~\cite{blalock2020state} provided a survey of neural network pruning and observed that many reported benefits of pruning are inflated by non-standard baselines. Our work builds on these contributions by developing an end-to-end verification taxonomy from data splits to FlatBuffer binaries.

Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} formalizes our 7-dimensional verification protocol. Each dimension targets a distinct vulnerability in the TinyML lifecycle:

\subsection{D1: Data-Level Isolation Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem:} Data contamination across training, validation, and test boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Predicate ($\mathcal{P}_1$):} $(\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}} = \emptyset) \land (\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}} \cap \mathcal{D}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset) \land (\mu_{\text{scaler}} = \text{Fit}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}})) \land (\theta^* = \arg\max_{\theta} \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}))$.
    \item \textbf{Executable Check:} Disjoint index assertion and scaler fitting provenance check.
    \item \textbf{Artifact:} Data split index arrays and serialized \texttt{scaler.pkl}.
    \item \textbf{Pass Condition:} Zero overlapping indices; calibration batches strictly drawn from $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$.
    \item \textbf{Case Result:} \textbf{PASS} ($22,399$ train, $22,399$ val, $11,200$ test).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{D2: Serialized Binary Integrity Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem:} Training-time in-memory metrics diverging from final disk FlatBuffer behavior.
    \item \textbf{Predicate ($\mathcal{P}_2$):} Evaluated metrics must be computed strictly by loading the final serialized \texttt{.tflite} file from disk into the interpreter.
    \item \textbf{Executable Check:} Automated single-sample evaluation loop on disk \texttt{.tflite} files.
    \item \textbf{Case Result:} \textbf{RESOLVED 16 DISCREPANCIES} (up to $7.82\%$ Macro F1 shift).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{D3: Quantization Graph Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem:} Silent floating-point fallback operators in quantized models.
    \item \textbf{Predicate ($\mathcal{P}_3$):} $\forall t \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{tensors}}, \text{dtype}(t) \in \{\text{int8}, \text{int32}, \text{uint8}\} \land \sum_{t \in \mathcal{G}} \mathbb{I}(\text{dtype}(t) = \text{float32}) = 0$.
    \item \textbf{Executable Check:} Iterating over \texttt{interpreter.get\_tensor\_details()} and \texttt{\_get\_ops\_details()}.
    \item \textbf{Case Result:} \textbf{PASS} (all 4 INT8 models verified \texttt{FULL\_INT8} with 0 float32 tensors).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{D4: Sparsity and Storage Decoupling Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem:} Conflating zero-valued weights with physical on-disk file compression.
    \item \textbf{Predicate ($\mathcal{P}_4$):} $\text{StorageCompression} = \text{FileSize}(\text{pruned}) / \text{FileSize}(\text{dense}) \ne 1 - \text{WeightSparsity}$.
    \item \textbf{Executable Check:} Comparing raw weight zero-counts against \texttt{os.path.getsize()} on FlatBuffers.
    \item \textbf{Case Result:} \textbf{CONFIRMED DECOUPLING} ($73.34\%$ zero weights produced $3,920$\,Bytes on disk vs. $3,892$\,Bytes unpruned).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{D5: Computational Accounting Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem:} Layer-wise MAC counts reported inconsistently or conflated with hardware cycles.
    \item \textbf{Predicate ($\mathcal{P}_5$):} $\text{ActiveMACs} = \sum_{l=1}^L \sum_{i,j} \mathbb{I}(w_{ij}^{(l)} \ne 0) \le \text{DenseMACs}$.
    \item \textbf{Case Result:} \textbf{VERIFIED EXACT} ($384$ dense MACs; $96$ active MACs at $75\%$ pruning; $160$ and $304$ MACs for student models).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{D6: Timing Protocol Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem:} Host latency improperly cited as embedded microcontroller WCET.
    \item \textbf{Predicate ($\mathcal{P}_6$):} $(\text{Warmup} \ge 100) \land (\text{Batch} = 1) \land (\text{Timer} \in \{\text{perf\_counter}, \text{esp\_timer}\}) \land (\text{Tier} \in \{\text{HOST}, \text{MCU}\})$.
    \item \textbf{Case Result:} \textbf{VERIFIED PASS} ($0.82$--$1.69\,\si{\micro\second}$ host latency on x86\_64; $64.55$--$89.90\,\si{\micro\second}$ physical latency on ESP32-D0WD-V3; zero formal WCET claimed).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{D7: Runtime Non-Leakage and Deployment Scoping}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem:} Ground-truth label leakage in dynamic controllers or ungrounded hardware claims.
    \item \textbf{Predicate ($\mathcal{P}_7$):} $\text{Signature}(\text{SelectModel}) = f(\mathbf{x}, D, W) \land y \notin \text{Scope}(\text{SelectModel})$.
    \item \textbf{Case Result:} \textbf{VERIFIED PASS} (zero ground-truth access during runtime scheduling).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ1: Discrepancy Prevalence and Defect Categorization}
Applying our protocol across all 12 candidate models identified \textbf{20 numerical discrepancies} (Table~\ref{tab:discrepancies}), categorized into distinct defect modes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Defect Mode 1 (Serialization Drift, 10 items):} Occurred when training scripts logged in-memory validation metrics prior to final fine-tuning or quantization calibration. Corrected by evaluating the finalized on-disk FlatBuffer directly ($\Delta \text{Acc} \le 3.26\%$).
    \item \textbf{Defect Mode 2 (In-Memory vs. True Integer Arithmetic, 2 items):} In-memory evaluation simulated quantization via fake-quantization operators rather than executing true integer arithmetic, producing a $7.82\%$ Macro F1 discrepancy in \texttt{mlp\_14f\_int8}.
    \item \textbf{Defect Mode 3 (Discretization Rounding, 8 items):} Minor parameter rounding during FlatBuffer conversion ($\Delta \le 0.19\%$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ2: Quantization Verification Results}
Low-level tensor auditing confirmed that all 4 INT8 candidate models (\texttt{mlp\_14f\_int8}, \texttt{mlp\_12f\_int8}, \texttt{student\_a\_8\_4\_int8}, \texttt{student\_b\_16\_4\_int8}) are verified as \textbf{\texttt{FULL\_INT8}}. The execution graphs contain exactly $0$ float32 tensors, executing pure integer \texttt{FULLY\_CONNECTED} and \texttt{SOFTMAX} operators.

\subsection{RQ3: Sparsity vs. Storage Decoupling Findings}
Visual inspection of pruned models revealed that while $75\%$ magnitude pruning achieved $73.34\%$ numerical zero weights ($298$ zeroes) and reduced theoretical active MACs from $384$ to $96$ ($75.0\%$ compute reduction), the serialized FlatBuffer file size remained dense at $3,920$\,Bytes ($+28$\,B over unpruned baseline due to metadata). The verification framework formally corrected the terminology to ``computational sparsity without demonstrated storage compression.''

\subsection{RQ4: Threshold Optimization Leakage Bias}
We conducted an empirical ablation evaluating gating thresholds calibrated on validation data versus optimized directly on test data. Selecting thresholds on test data produced an artificial optimistic accuracy bias of \textbf{$+1.80\%$}, demonstrating that split-isolated calibration is mandatory to prevent inflated performance claims.

\subsection{Physical On-Device Deployment Validation Case Study}
To demonstrate the artifact verification protocol across the final deployment transformation boundary ($\text{FlatBuffer} \rightarrow \text{C-Header} \rightarrow \text{Bare-Metal MCU Binary}$), the four verified \texttt{FULL\_INT8} candidate models were deployed to an ESP32-D0WD-V3 microcontroller (Xtensa LX6 dual-core @ $240\,\text{MHz}$, $4\,\text{MB}$ Flash, $320\,\text{KB}$ SRAM). Isolated single-sample execution latency was measured via \path{esp_timer_get_time()} across $24,000$ iterations ($3$ rounds $\times 2,000$ iterations per model):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{student\_a\_8\_4\_int8} ($176$ params): ESP32 Mean = $64.55\,\si{\micro\second}$, P95 = $69.00\,\si{\micro\second}$ (Host x86: $1.02\,\si{\micro\second}$, Slowdown: $63.3\times$).
    \item \texttt{student\_b\_16\_4\_int8} ($328$ params): ESP32 Mean = $72.96\,\si{\micro\second}$, P95 = $83.00\,\si{\micro\second}$ (Host x86: $0.98\,\si{\micro\second}$, Slowdown: $74.5\times$).
    \item \texttt{mlp\_12f\_int8} ($380$ params): ESP32 Mean = $76.77\,\si{\micro\second}$, P95 = $83.00\,\si{\micro\second}$ (Host x86: $1.00\,\si{\micro\second}$, Slowdown: $76.8\times$).
    \item \texttt{mlp\_14f\_int8} ($412$ params): ESP32 Mean = $89.90\,\si{\micro\second}$, P95 = $95.00\,\si{\micro\second}$ (Host x86: $1.43\,\si{\micro\second}$, Slowdown: $62.9\times$).
\end{itemize}
This physical deployment case study highlights two crucial methodological findings: (1) physical ESP32 execution provides an additional empirical deployment tier in the case study, establishing an observed host-to-ESP32 latency slowdown ratio between $62.9\times$ and $76.8\times$ without conflating host simulation timing with embedded execution; and (2) low-level memory inspection verified that TensorFlow Lite Micro \texttt{MicroAllocator} committed $916\,\text{Bytes}$ of static working tensor memory with zero runtime heap allocations.

\section{Scope and Generalization}
\subsection{General Principles vs. Case Implementation}
We explicitly distinguish general verification principles from our specific case implementation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{General Principle:} Deployment metrics must be evaluated directly on compiled disk binaries. \textit{Case Implementation:} Evaluated on TensorFlow Lite FlatBuffers via Python C-API bindings.
    \item \textbf{General Principle:} Execution dtypes must be audited for zero floating-point fallback. \textit{Case Implementation:} Parsed TFLite FlatBuffer tensor details.
    \item \textbf{General Principle:} Sparsity must be distinguished from storage compression. \textit{Case Implementation:} Evaluated dense FlatBuffer representation of pruned MLPs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Threats to Validity}
\subsection{Internal Validity}
All verification scripts were executed deterministically with fixed random seeds ($\text{seed}=42$). The audit pipeline parses exact binary headers using official TensorFlow Lite APIs.

\subsection{External Validity}
Our case study evaluated a suite of multi-layer perceptron models for multi-sensor diagnostic classification. While convolutional and recurrent topologies exhibit different operator graphs, the principles of binary inspection, data isolation, and timing protocols generalize across all TinyML domains.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single Case Study Scope:} Evaluated on one end-to-end multi-sensor diagnostic pipeline; multi-application cross-domain auditing remains future work.
    \item \textbf{Model Topology Focus:} Evaluated on feedforward MLPs; CNNs and transformers require future parser extensions.
    \item \textbf{Runtime Specificity:} Implemented for TensorFlow Lite FlatBuffers; alternative runtimes (microTVM, ONNX Runtime Mobile) are unverified.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Latency vs. Formal WCET:} While physical single-sample latency was measured on 240\,MHz ESP32 silicon ($64.55\text{--}89.90\,\si{\micro\second}$), these reflect empirical on-device distributions and do not establish formal static Worst-Case Execution Time (WCET) bounds.
    \item \textbf{Energy Profiling Boundary:} Physical power dissipation on silicon was not measured with hardware power analyzers.
    \item \textbf{Single Engine Domain:} Data reflects physical dynamometer recordings from one engine platform.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Bias Demonstration:} The $+1.80\%$ threshold leakage bias is specific to the evaluated multi-fidelity pipeline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproducibility and Artifact Availability}
All verification scripts (\path{scripts/phase4_5_verification.py}), audited model FlatBuffers, physical ESP32 PlatformIO deployment firmware (\path{phase5/firmware/}), and discrepancy resolution logs are publicly archived in the repository.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented an artifact-driven empirical verification protocol and defect taxonomy for compiled TinyML deployment artifacts. By formalizing verification criteria across seven core dimensions and auditing 12 candidate models, we demonstrated the critical necessity of inspecting serialized artifacts, resolving 20 numerical discrepancies and exposing sparsity-storage decoupling. Our protocol establishes actionable software engineering procedures to support reproducible benchmarking and defensible deployment claims in edge artificial intelligence research.
